Your enquiry

The contact form sends your name or band name, email address, selected service, optional track count and project message, including any links or personal information you choose to include.

Name, email address, service and message are required to submit the form; track count is optional. You are not obliged to contact me. Without the required fields, the form cannot submit your enquiry. You can also contact me directly by email.

I use this information to understand your project, respond and discuss potential services.

Where processing is necessary to take steps towards a contract with you at your request, the legal basis is GDPR Article 6(1)(b). For other correspondence, including enquiries made on behalf of a company or another person who would be the contracting party, the legal basis is my legitimate interest in handling enquiries and communicating with potential clients under Article 6(1)(f).

Website operation and security

GitHub Pages hosts the public website and logs visitor IP addresses for security. Cloudflare provides website-related services, including the portfolio API and media, contact-form processing and protection against abuse.

These providers process technical request information. Cloudflare request records can include IP addresses, request times, requested hostnames and paths, HTTP methods, response information and country information.

I use technical request information and security controls to keep the website functioning, investigate problems and prevent potential spam, automated abuse and excessive requests. The legal basis for this processing is my legitimate interest in operating and protecting the website under GDPR Article 6(1)(f).

Turnstile and rate limiting

Cloudflare Turnstile loads only when you first interact with the contact form, for example by focusing a field or tapping the form, to protect it against automated submissions. Simply viewing the homepage does not load Turnstile. Turnstile processes technical signals such as IP addresses, browser information, connection characteristics and the website origin. It supplies a verification token that the server checks with Cloudflare.

Cloudflare processes these signals on my behalf to provide website protection. Cloudflare also acts as an independent controller when processing signals to improve Turnstile’s bot-detection capabilities, as explained in its Turnstile Privacy Addendum.

Cloudflare’s edge rate limiter uses IP addresses. The contact form also creates a daily-changing pseudonymous identifier derived from the IP address for rate limiting.

The contact counters stored in Cloudflare D1 contain that identifier, counts and expiry times, together with a site-wide daily count. These counter records do not contain submitted enquiry messages or contact details.

Automated security checks may reject a submission or temporarily limit further attempts. You can contact me directly by email instead.

Who receives information?

GitHub Pages handles requests for the public website. Cloudflare processes website requests, contact-form submissions and security checks, and serves portfolio-related content.

Resend receives the enquiry information needed to deliver the contact-form email directly to my Microsoft-hosted Outlook mailbox. Microsoft processes the email to provide the mailbox service.

I am the only person who routinely accesses and responds to AS Mixes enquiries. The service providers involved, and their relevant service providers, may process information as necessary to provide their services. Where providers process information for their own purposes, their privacy notices explain that processing.

International data transfers

These providers may process personal data outside the European Economic Area, including in the United States.

Resend’s primary processing operations take place in the United States. Cloudflare, GitHub and Microsoft also describe processing in the United States and other countries where they or their service providers operate.

Cloudflare’s and Resend’s data-processing terms include the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses for transfers requiring those safeguards. GitHub’s and Microsoft’s privacy statements also describe international-transfer safeguards. The providers describe reliance on adequacy arrangements, including the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, where applicable to the particular transfer.

Further information about the relevant arrangements is available in the provider documents linked below. You can also contact contact@asmixes.com to request information about the safeguards applicable to your information and how to obtain a copy.

How long is information kept?

Enquiry emails and related correspondence are kept while needed to respond, discuss a potential project or manage an ongoing project.

Enquiries that do not result in a project are normally reviewed within six months of the last meaningful correspondence and deleted when no longer needed. Information is kept longer only where there is a specific continuing purpose, such as an active project discussion, a dispute or a legal retention requirement.

When a project goes ahead, relevant correspondence is kept while needed to deliver the agreed work, handle follow-up questions or resolve contractual issues. Records that must be retained by law are kept for the applicable legal period.

This is a manual review process, not a promise that every copy is automatically erased exactly six months after receipt. Microsoft’s applicable recovery and backup arrangements may continue to apply after messages are deleted from the mailbox.

Resend states that email and log data on its Free plan is retained for 30 days while the account is active. Its documentation separately describes seven-day backups and deletion of remaining customer data within 90 days after account termination. These periods do not determine how long an email already delivered to my Outlook mailbox is kept.

D1 per-identifier rate-limit counters expire at the end of a 10-minute window, and the site-wide counter expires at the end of its daily window. Cleanup is scheduled hourly and deletes up to 500 expired records per run. Expiry makes a record eligible for cleanup; it is not an exact deletion deadline or a guarantee about provider backups.

Cloudflare and GitHub retain provider-held technical and security records under their applicable retention arrangements, taking account of service operation, security and legal requirements. These records are separate from the D1 counters. A dashboard’s available reporting history does not establish an exact deletion date for all underlying data.

Browser storage and analytics

When you choose a colour theme, the website saves your preference in your browser’s local storage under asmixes-theme. The site sets no automatic expiry for this preference. You can remove it by clearing the relevant browser site data. The theme code does not send the saved value to the AS Mixes server.

AS Mixes uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to understand website traffic and performance. Cloudflare states that this service does not use cookies, local storage or browser fingerprinting to track individual visitors, and does not track visitors across its customers’ websites.

Turnstile also uses browser storage for verification. A local-storage entry named cf.turnstile.u has been observed under challenges.cloudflare.com. This storage is controlled by Cloudflare rather than by the website’s theme-preference code. Information about Turnstile’s processing is available in its Privacy Addendum.

Turnstile pre-clearance is disabled on this website.

You can manage browser storage through your browser’s site-data controls, including data associated with this website and Cloudflare’s challenge domain.

Your rights

Depending on the circumstances, you can request access to your personal data, correction, deletion, restriction of processing and data portability. You can object to processing based on legitimate interests.

Contact contact@asmixes.com to make a request.

You can complain to Estonia’s Data Protection Inspectorate (Andmekaitse Inspektsioon), or to the supervisory authority in the EU country where you live or work or where the alleged infringement occurred.
